# How closely does Cooldown Counter match Counter-Strike 2?

Reviewed 21 November 2026 against the defaults on main at commit `7327b42`. This review covers cooldowns issued in Valve matchmaking. It compares the tracker with Valve's published rules. No live account history or private matchmaking data was available for comparison.

The findings below describe the reviewed commit. For the implemented game rules, observed timers and existing-fleet upgrade instructions, see the [current data model](data-model.md#time-and-history).

## Verdict

**The tracker gets the four ordinary penalty durations right. It can predict the wrong one for a particular account.** The two clearest reasons are that the tracker keeps a separate level for each offence type and stops advancing that level when it reaches the last duration. Valve describes an ordinary competitive level attached to the account, and says that a seven-day penalty can represent level four or higher. These differences matter even with perfect timestamps and a complete history. [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq] · [Tracker history selection][reports] · [Prediction engine][engine]

**Treat the VACLive and GriefReports forecasts as fleet estimates.** The VACLive default stops at 30 days, while Valve describes repeat VACNet restrictions lasting up to 180 days. The current griefing guidance confirms that excessive reports can lead to a cooldown, but gives no griefing-specific timetable. There is enough evidence to reject a general claim of game accuracy. There is too little to replace both ladders with exact new sequences. [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq] · [Seeded policies][seeds] · [Valve's historical griefing update][grief-cap]

## What is being compared

The unit of comparison is the next **issued cooldown** on one Steam account. A match abandonment, a vote kick, a player report and a cooldown notice are different observations. In particular, one kick or report does not establish that Valve issued a penalty. The app takes an operator's report of an issued event as input. It does not read a Steam account's enforcement level or decide when Valve's detection threshold was crossed. Two fleets tracking the same SteamID can also hold different histories. [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq] · [Data model][model] · [Report service][reports]

For the game rules, the strongest evidence here is Valve's current English CS2 support article and its CS2 release notes. Older CS:GO sources are marked as historical. They help explain the expiry clock and the origin of griefing penalties, but cannot by themselves establish a current CS2 threshold. For the tracker, the evidence is the policy seeded for a new fleet, the history query used by the preview and the Python functions that assign steps and forecast reductions. Existing fleets may have edited their policies. [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq] · [Seeded policies][seeds] · [Report service][reports] · [Prediction engine][engine]

One historical source needs care. In February 2013, Valve developer Vitaliy illustrated how a seven-day cooldown could raise the level from four to five, then showed recovery after expiry. The post closes by associating level three with two hours, contradicting its own opening table. The duration calculations below use the current CS2 table, which assigns 24 hours to level three. [Archived Valve developer post][historical-clock] · [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq]

## Findings for each seeded offence

| Fleet default | Durations seeded, in order | Comparison with Valve's published behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abandoning | 30 min, 2 h, 24 h, 7 d | **Duration match.** Valve includes abandoning an unfinished match and failing to reconnect in time among the reasons for an ordinary cooldown. The tracker can reproduce the progression for successive abandons below the cap when the account history is known. Starting a separate abandon level can miss a prior qualifying penalty for another reason. [Steam Support][faq] |
| Team Damage | 30 min, 2 h, 24 h, 7 d | **Real reason, partial match.** Valve fixed a CS2 bug that counted a player's Molotov self-damage towards a team-damage penalty. Earlier CS:GO notes distinguish damage in a short interval from excessive damage over the whole match. Neither source establishes a current universal damage threshold or a separate team-damage escalation level. The numbers match the ordinary duration table. [CS2 fix][team-cs2] · [Historical team-damage rules][team-csgo] · [Steam Support][faq] |
| Kicking | 30 min, 2 h, 24 h, 7 d | **Incomplete reason coverage.** The seed describes being vote-kicked. Valve distinguishes repeated removal by vote from excessive use of kick votes against others, including voting for the kick. An isolated vote-kick is insufficient evidence of an issued cooldown. The ordinary durations match, with the same account-history problem as abandoning. [Steam Support][faq] · [Seed definition][seeds] |
| GriefReports | 2 h, 1 d, 7 d, 30 d | **Exact sequence unsupported.** Valve confirms a sanction for excessive griefing reports. In July 2021 it described a warning followed by escalating cooldowns. In September 2021 it raised the CS:GO maximum from one day to 14 days. The 30-day seed exceeds that historical limit. The CS2 FAQ gives a general non-VACNet table without explaining how the older griefing rule carries forward. The present griefing ceiling, intermediate steps, relationship to ordinary state and recovery rate remain unresolved. [Steam Support][faq] · [July 2021 notes][grief-warning] · [September 2021 notes][grief-cap] |
| VACLive | 20 h, 7 d, 30 d | **Range mismatch.** The closest published mechanism is a temporary VACNet irregular-gameplay restriction. Valve gives an upper duration of 180 days for repeats. The same English FAQ gives a 20-hour minimum in its duration section and a one-day minimum in its trigger descriptions. Neither passage establishes a fixed first duration, a 7-day second duration, or weekly decay. The app cannot select a duration above 30 days under this seed. [Steam Support][faq] · [Seed definition][seeds] |

The CS2 client's English notices distinguish team damage at round start, excessive team damage, repeated removal by vote, abuse of kick votes, grief reports, and VACNet restrictions for the flagged player or a party member. These labels give operators a way to record the reason shown in the game. The cited file is a pinned third-party mirror of Valve's client text. The presence of a label establishes neither its current trigger threshold nor its penalty duration. [CS2 client notices][client]

The five labels also leave coverage gaps. Steam Support includes inactivity and failure to stay connected. A fleet can add a label, but doing so starts another independent history. Valve also documented a cooldown when an official competitive server kicks a player for excessive in-game suicides. For Premier, Valve fixed a case where a single abandon during map voting produced multiple cooldowns. Counting a repeat observation once agrees with that example. The fix gives no separate Premier duration table. [Steam Support][faq] · [CS2 February 2024 release notes][other-kick] · [Premier abandon fix][premier] · [Data model][model]

VACNet restrictions can apply to the flagged player and to players who queued with them. Steam Support describes all-mode restrictions and a CS Rating adjustment in that context. The tracker records an event for the selected account and shows an active-until time. Its event model has no affected modes, party relationship or rating effect. An active badge therefore cannot answer which queues the game will allow. A permanent VAC ban and a temporary VACNet cooldown also need distinct treatment. [Steam Support cooldown FAQ][faq] · [Event model][events] · [Account projections][queries]

## How escalation and recovery differ

| Question | Tracker on main | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which history counts? | Preview reads events for one account and one stable offence type across policy versions. | Valve describes ordinary probation and repeat penalties at the account level. A shared level for qualifying ordinary reasons is the strongest reading. The FAQ does not enumerate every mixed-reason pair or show that griefing and VACNet share this state. [Steam Support][faq] · [History query][reports] |
| What happens after level four? | The predictor caps the next assigned step at the number of configured durations. Further ordinary events can keep returning seven days while storing step four. | Valve explicitly allows a seven-day duration to represent level four or higher. A Valve developer's historical worked example advances to level five while retaining seven days. The app loses information needed to forecast recovery. Appending one extra seven-day row only moves the point where it loses that information. [Steam Support][faq] · [Valve developer example][historical-clock] · [Prediction engine][engine] |
| When does a level fall? | By default, each completed seven-day window removes one stored level. The next event advances one from that reduced state. A calendar marker can therefore show a reduction without the next penalty falling to the duration displayed for the reduced state. | Valve's current example agrees that an account reduced to level two gets 24 hours on its next infraction. The historical developer explanation counts the clean week from expiry. The current FAQ does not define the server's exact boundary, how inactivity counts, or how overlapping restrictions affect the clock. [Steam Support][faq] · [Historical clock example][historical-clock] · [Forecast and prediction][engine] |
| What does the calendar assume? | A week is seven elapsed periods of 24 hours. For overlaps of the same type, the latest start supplies the saved level and the latest expiry supplies the clock, even if those belong to different events. | This is a reproducible tracker rule. The sources reviewed do not establish this overlap calculation for CS2. The optional full-reset and no-decay modes are also fleet choices. Publishing a custom policy changes future assessments while preserving the policy and expiry saved on existing events. [Prediction engine][engine] · [Data model][model] |
| What if history is missing or later corrected? | An empty history starts at step one. A manual override must select a configured step. A backdated event leaves previously saved assessments intact. | An operator's first entry can concern an account already on probation. Even a known seven-day total cannot identify its exact underlying level. Valve also says its servers can roll back an incorrect automated restriction. An immutable assessment is useful for audit, but the tracker needs a way to record evidence that supersedes it if it is to forecast corrected state. [Steam Support][faq] · [Data model][model] · [Event model][events] |

## Worked comparisons

The examples assume ordinary issued penalties, known prior history and no unlisted intervening events unless stated. The tracker calculations below use the unedited defaults. The game-side results follow from the published rules.

1. **A level falls but the next penalty is still 24 hours.** Start with a saved level-three ordinary penalty. One clean week after expiry, the tracker reduces stored level three to two. A new offence immediately advances it to three, for 24 hours. This reproduces Valve's example and illustrates why the stored level and the next penalty need separate labels. [Steam Support][faq] · [Prediction engine][engine]
2. **Switching reasons can underpredict by 90 minutes.** A previously clear account gets a 30-minute abandon penalty. An hour after it expires, the account receives a qualifying cooldown for being kicked repeatedly. Its Kicking history is empty, so the tracker predicts 30 minutes again. Applying the published account-level rule gives level two and two hours. The scenario starts from an issued second penalty. It assumes nothing about how many votes trigger one. [Steam Support][faq] · [History query][reports]
3. **Two consecutive seven-day penalties can hide a six-day forecast error.** Take five ordinary penalties of the same type, each issued an hour after the previous one expires. Both progressions give 30 minutes, 2 hours, 24 hours, 7 days and 7 days. The tracker saves level four on the fifth event. After two clean weeks it computes 4 - 2 + 1 = 3 and predicts 24 hours. The historical Valve progression saves level five. Applying the weekly reduction gives 5 - 2 + 1 = 4 and seven days. Both accounts of the durations agree until the recovery forecast. [Steam Support][faq] · [Valve developer example][historical-clock] · [Prediction engine][engine]
4. **A visible timer can exceed every available override.** Suppose a VACNet notice shows 90 days remaining when recorded. This example uses a hypothetical timer within the published range. The longest VACLive step is 30 days. The report would fail validation because the observed remaining time exceeds the selected total. For a shorter timer, choosing a longer configured step may admit the observation while setting expiry to the entered start plus that longer step. Later readings leave that expiry in place. This can affect both the active badge and the date recovery supposedly begins. [Steam Support][faq] · [Report service][reports] · [Observation validation][events] · [Account projections][queries]

I ran the repository's 47 pure predictor tests in Docker against the reviewed source. All passed. I also ran the predictor with the seeded durations for the same-type progression, the two-week recovery, the level-three weekly reduction and an empty Kicking history. The results above combine those outputs with inspection of the report service's history filter. These checks establish how the app behaves. Measuring game prediction accuracy would require paired predictions and actual game notices. [Predictor tests][tests] · [History query][reports]

## What to change if the goal is game accuracy

1. **Preserve observations that contradict a prediction.** Allow a timer reading and its observation time to be saved when the level, original total or expiry is unknown. Store the predicted expiry separately from an observed or estimated expiry, including the timer's display precision. Preserve the original assessment when later evidence corrects it. An operator should be able to enter the hypothetical long VACNet reading above without first editing fleet rules.
2. **Separate the reason from the ordinary competitive level.** Keep the reason on the event. Maintain a shared ordinary progression for reasons supported by the evidence, and map its level to a duration separately. Four displayed durations need not impose a maximum stored level. Test the mixed-reason and consecutive seven-day examples above. Keep existing assessments pinned to their original policy. Publish any game-based policy as a new version and preserve custom fleet rules.
3. **Represent uncertainty in the two special cases.** For VACNet, show an observed timer or the supported bounds with their source, along with self or party attribution where known. For grief reports, record the notice and distinguish a warning from an issued restriction. Leave intermediate stages and decay unknown until there is evidence. For an imported seven-day ordinary cooldown without earlier history, the strongest initial statement is level four or higher. An exact recovery date would need more history.
4. **Validate forecasts against naturally occurring notices.** With permission, capture redacted notices, the displayed reason, mode, UTC observation time, timer precision, and whether prior history is complete. Save the prediction before the next notice, then compare duration and release time. Start with mixed ordinary reasons, repeat seven-day sanctions, and VACNet self versus party notices. Report counts and errors separately by mechanism. A single observed duration can disprove a fixed prediction without establishing the full schedule.

The project already records ownership of the initial ladders and independent decay as an open question. This review supplies evidence for that decision. The remaining product choice is whether a fleet wants its own editable rules, game-based estimates, or both. Until then, the ordinary duration table is a sound reference and the tracker forecasts should retain their model-assumption label.
